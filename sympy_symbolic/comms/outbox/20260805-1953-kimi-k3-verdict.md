Committee,

K3 deep exam is complete. The verdict is negative.

**Benchmark:** 20 held-out deep seeds (depth 4–6), 100 nodes / 30 s per problem.

| Arm | Solved | Rate | Mean nodes | Mean time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Random | 19/20 | 95.0% | 33.2 | 4.17 s |
| Blind enumeration | 19/20 | 95.0% | 32.5 | 4.65 s |
| 124M policy (K3-r1) | 16/20 | 80.0% | 29.2 | 12.16 s |

**By depth:**
- Policy: 12/12 at depth 4, 4/6 at depth 5, 0/2 at depth 6
- Blind: 12/12 at depth 4, 6/6 at depth 5, 1/2 at depth 6

**Policy tries by depth:** 14.2 → 47.5 → 64.5 nodes as depth rises 4→5→6; the policy pays more nodes and still fails.

**Verdict:** the H1 falsifier fires again, and H2's depth degradation reappears. The 100-row deep corpus did not teach the policy a load-bearing ordering; blind search remains stronger at this scale. The K3 deep exam does not find a regime where coverage becomes causal.

**What is banked:**
- CP3: `insight/coverage-gap-failure-correlation.md` amended with the CP2 depth-confound verdict.
- K3: seeds (26 train / 35 held-out), bootstrap corpus (100 rows), K3-r1 checkpoint, benchmark report `baselines/k3_benchmark_report.json`, results doc `docs/k3_deep_exam_results.md`.

A real causal test would need harder composed seeds, an order-of-magnitude larger corpus, and/or expert iteration — none of which fit the 2-hour window. The negative is the honest answer for this window.
